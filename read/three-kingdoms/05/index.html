The Pass and the River

Tong Pass, 211 CE — An announced march through Guanzhong brought ten warlords to arms at a single pass; the war that followed was won by a saddle held against arrows, a wall of frozen sand, and one letter full of erasures.

On our sources
02 / 16 In the sources
Zhong You was ordered to lead the way through Guanzhong, with Xiahou Yuan coming out of Hedong to join him. The clerk Gao Rou warned that the western generals would read the Hanzhong campaign as a cover for conquering them, and rise in self-defence. He was not listened to, and it happened as he said.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1, Annals of Emperor Wu; Wei shu 24, Biography of Gao Rou
03 / 16 In the sources
The roll of the coalition varies slightly between sources; the Zizhi Tongjian names Han Sui, Hou Xuan, Li Kan, Cheng Yin, Zhang Heng, Liang Xing, Cheng Yi, Yang Qiu and others. Ma Chao rose knowing his father Ma Teng sat at Ye as a hostage of the court; Ma Teng and his family were put to death the following year.
Zizhi Tongjian, scroll 66; Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1
04 / 16 In the sources
The answer is from the annals, translated closely. Cao Ren was sent ahead to contain the coalition with a firm camp and no engagement, until the chancellor himself came up.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1, Annals of Emperor Wu
05 / 16 In the sources
By Cao Cao's own later account, the grand display at Tong Pass was meant to draw every defender south and leave the upper river unwatched. The standoff at the gate was bait for a crossing.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1, Annals of Emperor Wu; Zizhi Tongjian, scroll 66
06 / 16 In the sources
The secret crossing stands in the annals as a single sentence — two generals, a night march, and a camp on the far bank. The whole campaign turned on it.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1, Annals of Emperor Wu
07 / 16 In the sources
The whole scene is in Xu Chu's biography: the boatman killed at his oar, the saddle raised as a shield, and Cao Cao's words once across. The annals add that Ding Fei loosed cattle and horses on the bank as bait, and the rebels broke ranks to plunder them; his own biography says he took five flying arrows beside the boat.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 18, Biographies of Xu Chu and Ding Fei; Wei shu 1
08 / 16 In the sources
The device is in Lou Gui's biography: no stone, no timber, sand walls hardened with poured water in the cold, finished in one night. Against cavalry on open ground, it gave the army a back to fight from.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 23, Biography of Lou Gui
09 / 16 In the sources
The annals record the offer and the refusal. The peace that was refused would be asked for again within the month — and the second time it would be granted, though never meant.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1, Annals of Emperor Wu; Zizhi Tongjian, scroll 66
10 / 16 In the sources
“Part them, nothing more” is Jia Xu's recorded answer. The annals add Cao Cao's one-word reply — “understood” — and the pretence that followed: peace agreed to, and never intended.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 10, Biography of Jia Xu; Wei shu 1
11 / 16 In the sources
The meeting is in the annals: old acquaintances on horseback between the lines, talking for hours of everything but the war. When Ma Chao afterwards asked what had been said, Han Sui answered: nothing. The nothing began to work.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1, Annals of Emperor Wu; Wei shu 10
12 / 16 In the sources
The doctored letter is in Jia Xu's biography and in the annals: many erasures and corrections, made to seem Han Sui's own doing. The stratagem cost one sheet of paper; the suspicion it needed was already planted.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 10, Biography of Jia Xu; Wei shu 1
13 / 16 In the sources
The order of battle is from the annals: light troops as bait first, then the picked men striking both flanks. Cheng Yi and Li Kan died in the rout; Ma Chao and Han Sui fled to Liang Province, and Guanzhong, says the same sentence, was pacified.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1, Annals of Emperor Wu
14 / 16 In the sources
Yang Fu's warning and its sequel are in his own biography: Ma Chao returned at the head of Qiang and Hu, Longshang rose to him, and two years later Ji city was taken and its provincial officials killed. Guanzhong had to be pacified twice; the second time was Xiahou Yuan's generals' work.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 25, Biography of Yang Fu; Wei shu 9
15 / 16 In the sources
The chancellor's own recapitulation is in the annals: the grand show at Tong Pass to empty the upper river, the secret crossing at Puban, the refusal of peace to breed arrogance, then the stroke. His generals confessed they had not come near to seeing it.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1, Annals of Emperor Wu
16 / 16 In the sources
Yang Qiu's surrender is in the annals: he was received back into his commandery, a cheap peace for an unquiet frontier. Zhang Lu held Hanzhong until 215; the march announced as a pretext became, in the end, a conquest.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1; Zizhi Tongjian, scroll 66

Myth Check

The novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms is a masterpiece — and not a source. Where its famous scenes depart from the histories, we leave them out of the story and put them here.

The story you may know

Cao Cao cut off his beard and cast off his robe to escape Ma Chao.

What the sources say

No official history has it. What the records give for the crossing is dramatic enough without it: arrows like rain, a dead boatman, Xu Chu raising a saddle as a shield and cutting the clinging men from the hull, Ding Fei scattering cattle and horses as a bait. The beard and the robe are chapter 58 of the novel.

Romance of the Three Kingdoms, ch. 58; absent from Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1 and Wei shu 18
The story you may know

Xu Chu stripped off his armour and fought Ma Chao in single combat.

What the sources say

The duel of chapter 59 is the novel's. Xu Chu's recorded part at Tong Pass was defensive — carrying his lord across under arrows — and no single combat between the two appears in any history.

Sanguozhi, Wei shu 18, Biography of Xu Chu
The story you may know

Ma Chao rebelled to avenge his father, whom Cao Cao lured to the capital and murdered.

What the sources say

The order in the histories is the reverse: Ma Teng had lived at Ye since 208 as a court officer and hostage; it was Ma Chao's rising that forfeited his family, and Ma Teng was executed in 212, the year after the rebellion began. Later historians blamed Ma Chao for staking his father on a war.

Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1 & Shu shu 6; Zizhi Tongjian, scrolls 66–67
The story you may know

Han Sui turned traitor at Tong Pass and surrendered to Cao Cao.

What the sources say

No defection is recorded at the battle: the coalition quarrelled, broke, and its two heads fled together. Han Sui fought on as a freebooter of the west for three more years and was killed by his own generals in 215; what reached Cao Cao's court was his head, not his submission.

Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1; Dianlüe, in Pei Songzhi's commentary
