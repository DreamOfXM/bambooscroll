Fire on the Yangtze

Red Cliff, 208 CE — One river. Three warlords. A fire that decided China would not be united for sixty years.
20 panels~8 min read free to readevery line sourced
01 / 20 Note
This episode is told only from the official histories: the Records of the Three Kingdoms, Pei Songzhi's fifth-century commentary, and the Zizhi Tongjian. The novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms is used nowhere as a source — it appears once, at the end, in the Myth Check, as a correction.
On our sources
02 / 20 In the sources
In the seventh month of 208 he marched against Liu Biao, governor of Jing Province. By the ninth month he was at Xiangyang.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1, Annals of Emperor Wu
03 / 20 In the sources
Liu Cong handed over Jing Province without a battle. Liu Bei, garrisoned at Fancheng, learned of it only when Cao Cao's army was already upon Xiangyang.
Sanguozhi, Shu shu 2, Biography of the Former Lord
04 / 20 In the sources
Jing Province's warships and its river troops passed to Cao Cao intact. The war would now be decided on water — by an army of northerners who, as Zhou Yu put it, had left their saddles to fight from ships.
Sanguozhi, Wu shu 9 (Zhou Yu's memorial); Wei shu 1
05 / 20 In the sources
His column moved barely ten li a day because of the refugees. His advisers urged him to ride ahead and save himself. The quotation is verbatim from his biography.
Sanguozhi, Shu shu 2
06 / 20 In the sources
The annals record the distance and the speed. What happened at Changban was not a battle. It was a run-down.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1
07 / 20 In the sources
This is in Zhao Yun's biography, not in legend: “Yun held the weak child in his arms — the future lord — and protected Lady Gan.” One sentence in the record. The novel added four chapters.
Sanguozhi, Shu shu 6, Biography of Zhao Yun
08 / 20 In the sources
The challenge is verbatim from his biography. The pursuing cavalry stopped. No one crossed.
Sanguozhi, Shu shu 6, Biography of Zhang Fei
09 / 20 In the sources
Lu Su had argued for an alliance before Changban even happened. He met Liu Bei at Dangyang, and travelled back south with Zhuge Liang in his party.
Sanguozhi, Wu shu 9, Biography of Lu Su
10 / 20 In the sources
Zhuge Liang's audience with Sun Quan, and his argument that Cao Cao's army was exhausted and out of its element, are recorded in his own biography. The famous scene of him humiliating a hall of scholars is not — that is the novel's.
Sanguozhi, Shu shu 5, Biography of Zhuge Liang
11 / 20 Debated
The number is Cao Cao's own, from his letter to Sun Quan — “I now command eight hundred thousand marines” — preserved in the Jiangbiao zhuan quoted by Pei Songzhi. Zhou Yu's estimate of the real strength was far lower, and he said so in court. Treat all figures from this war as claims, not counts.
Jiangbiao zhuan, in Pei Songzhi's commentary to Wu shu 9; Zizhi Tongjian, scroll 65
12 / 20 In the sources
The four objections — unrest in the rear, untrained sailors, winter, and sickness — are recorded nearly word for word in his biography and in the Zizhi Tongjian.
Sanguozhi, Wu shu 9; Zizhi Tongjian, scroll 65
13 / 20 Commentary
The desk-cutting comes from the Jiangbiao zhuan, a Wu court chronicle quoted in Pei Songzhi's fifth-century commentary. The figure of thirty thousand men is from the Zizhi Tongjian, scroll 65. Both sit one step below the main text, and we mark them so.
Jiangbiao zhuan, in Pei Songzhi's commentary; Zizhi Tongjian, scroll 65
14 / 20 In the sources
Cheng Pu held the right wing under Zhou Yu; Lu Su came as a colonel. The force was small, and it knew it was small.
Sanguozhi, Wu shu 9
15 / 20 Debated
The exact site is still argued by scholars; the bank at modern Chibi in Hubei is the mainstream candidate, not a certainty. We mark it as debated rather than pretending otherwise.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1 & Wu shu 9; modern site studies
16 / 20 In the sources
Huang Gai's own words, from his biography: “The enemy's ships lie head to tail; they can be burned and driven off.” The connected ships are in the source — no trick required.
Sanguozhi, Wu shu 10, Biography of Huang Gai
17 / 20 In the sources
Huang Gai sent Cao Cao a letter offering to defect. The histories do not say Cao Cao believed it. They say he waited for the ships to arrive.
Sanguozhi, Wu shu 10
18 / 20 In the sources
“The wind was fierce; the fire spread and burned the camps on the shore.” The novel needed an altar and a summoned wind. The record only needs weather.
Sanguozhi, Wu shu 10
19 / 20 In the sources
“I fought Liu Bei at Red Cliff; it went badly. A great epidemic killed many of my officers and men, so I led the army home.” Note what the conqueror's own record does not mention: the fire.
Sanguozhi, Wei shu 1, Annals of Emperor Wu
20 / 20 Note
No one won China at Red Cliff. What the fire decided was that no one would — for sixty more years.

Four people carried this year. The sources disagree about almost everything else.

Cao Cao曹操
155–220
Chancellor of Han, master of the north. Came south to finish the empire in one campaign.
Liu Bei劉備
161–223
A claimant with a famous name and almost nothing else. Began this year in flight; ended it with a province.
Zhou Yu周瑜
175–210
Chief commander of Wu. Read Cao Cao's army correctly and burned it. Died of illness in 210, aged 36.
Zhuge Liang諸葛亮

Myth Check

The novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms is a masterpiece — and not a source. Where its famous scenes depart from the histories, we leave them out of the story and put them here.

The story you may know

Zhuge Liang borrowed arrows with straw boats.

What the sources say

No such event at Red Cliff appears in any source. A similar incident is attributed to Sun Quan at Ruxu in 213 CE — five years later, a different man, a different river — in the Weilüe quoted by Pei Songzhi.

Weilüe, in Pei Songzhi's commentary
The story you may know

Zhuge Liang summoned the east wind from an altar.

What the sources say

The sources give the wind to Huang Gai, not to magic: “the wind was fierce.” Winter southeasterlies on this reach of the Yangtze are a known local weather pattern. No altar required.

Sanguozhi, Wu shu 10
The story you may know

Pang Tong tricked Cao Cao into chaining his fleet.

What the sources say

The histories never place Pang Tong at Red Cliff. What they record is Huang Gai observing that the enemy ships lay head to tail — a formation he noticed, not a trap he set.

Sanguozhi, Wu shu 10
The story you may know

Zhuge Liang emptied a city to bluff Sima Yi.

What the sources say

Not this battle, not this decade. The story survives in the Guochong sanshi — quoted, then dismantled, by Pei Songzhi himself, who noted that Sima Yi was nowhere near the place and time.

Guochong sanshi, in Pei Songzhi's commentary, with his rebuttal
The story you may know

Zhou Yu died of rage at being outsmartened.

What the sources say

The histories record a commander of generous character who died of illness in 210, aged 36. His old colleague Cheng Pu said of him: “Being with Zhou Yu is like drinking fine wine — you are drunk before you know it.”

Sanguozhi, Wu shu 9; Pei Songzhi commentary
