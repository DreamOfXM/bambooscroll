The Last Campaign

Wuzhang Plains, 234 CE — Five roads over the Qinling in seven years, and every one of them died on the grain. The last ended against a general who would not fight — and a grave in the mountain, just big enough for a coffin.

In the spring of 228 Zhuge Liang came out by Qishan; Ma Su, sent to hold Jieting, abandoned the watercourse for the hill and was broken by Zhang He. The advance collapsed and the army withdrew.

The Sanguozhi says Ma Su was imprisoned and died there; the Tongjian and the Xiangyang ji say he was executed. Zhuge Liang mourned him, took the blame on himself, and was demoted three grades.
Sanguozhi, Shu shu 5 & Shu shu 9, Pei Songzhi commentary; Zizhi Tongjian 71

Zhuge Liang besieged Chencang, held by Hao Zhao with about a thousand men; after twenty days and every engine, supplies ran out and he withdrew. Wang Shuang pursued and was killed.

In 229 Zhuge Liang sent Chen Shi to take Wudu and Yinping; Liu Shan restored him to the chancellorship. It was the only expedition that left Shu holding new commanderies.

06 / 16 In the sources
In 231 Zhuge Liang besieged Qishan, transporting with the wooden ox; Sima Yi, now in command, kept to his camp. On the withdrawal Zhang He pursued to Mumen and was killed by crossbow fire.

In this episode

One man carried these seven years and five campaigns, and one man answered every one of them by refusing to fight. Neither lost the argument: the grain did, and then the body did.

Zhuge Liang諸葛亮
181–234
Chancellor of Shu-Han. Five expeditions north, one grave in Mount Dingjun, and an appraisal that praises the administrator first.
Sima Yi司馬懿
179–251
Wei's commander in the west from 231. Refused battle at the Wuzhang Plains, outlived his enemy, and left his house the empire.

Agreed the two-front war for 234, marched on Hefei's new city, and was turned back by an emperor and a sickness.

Myth Check

The novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms is a masterpiece — and not a source. Where its famous scenes depart from the histories, we leave them out of the story and put them here.

The story you may know

The Empty City: gates flung open, Zhuge Liang on the wall with a lute, and Sima Yi turning his army away from nothing.

What the sources say

The only source that carries it — Guo Chong's Three Matters — is quoted by Pei Songzhi precisely in order to refute it: Sima Yi was not at Yangping in those years, and the geography and the numbers do not stand. We quote it here, as a correction, and nowhere else.

Sanguozhi, Shu shu 5, Pei Songzhi commentary quoting and rejecting Guo Chong
The story you may know

Zhuge Liang was unbeatable in the field, and only fate stopped him at the walls of Chang'an.

What the sources say

Chen Shou's own appraisal is colder and kinder: able in governing armies, short in stratagem; his civil administration exceeded his generalship. The expeditions died on the grain road through the Qinling — and Sima Yi's refusal of battle was the correct answer to that, not cowardice.

Sanguozhi, Shu shu 5, appraisal; Zizhi Tongjian 72
The story you may know

Ma Su was beheaded at Zhuge Liang's order while Zhuge Liang wept — the scene exactly as everyone knows it.

What the sources say

The Sanguozhi says Ma Su was imprisoned and died there; the execution and the tears come from later telling. The histories disagree, and this site leaves the disagreement standing rather than choosing the better story.

Sanguozhi, Shu shu 5 & Shu shu 9, Pei Songzhi commentary; Zizhi Tongjian 71
